The Free AI Stack for Job Hunting in 2026 (Resume to Interview Prep)

2026-09-08 · ToolTested

Every job-hunting "AI hack" list is secretly an affiliate pitch. This is the free stack I actually use, in the order you'd actually use it.

Step 1: Tailor the resume (free chatbot)

Upload your current resume as text and paste the job description, then:

Rewrite my resume bullet points to mirror the language of this job
description. Keep every claim truthful — do not invent experience.
Return it as a table: original bullet | rewritten bullet | why.

The table matters. It forces the model to show its reasoning so you can reject anything that drifts into fiction.

Step 2: The ATS keyword pass

Same chatbot, second prompt:

List the hard skills and keywords from this job description, split into
"must have" and "nice to have". Then mark which ones appear in my resume.

Close the gaps in your experience honestly — a course, a side project, a tool you've actually touched. Never keyword-stuff.

Step 3: Cover letter without the cringe

The trick is constraining the AI. Ask for a 150-word letter built from three inputs: the company's actual product, one real accomplishment with a number, and one specific reason you want this job. Anything longer than 150 words forces the model to pad, and padding is what makes letters read as AI-written.

Step 4: Interview practice that isn't softball

Two tools that are free:

  • NotebookLM: upload the job description, your resume, and the company's "About" page. Ask it to interview you, role-play the hiring manager, and only ask questions grounded in those sources.
  • Any voice-capable chatbot: run a spoken mock interview. Speaking your answers exposes the difference between knowing something and being able to say it smoothly.

What to skip

Resume-builder sites with AI upsells, "AI resume graders" (they score you low to sell a rewrite), and LinkedIn AI messages — recruiters see thousands of those templates and recognize them instantly.

Verdict

The free stack beats the paid one for 90% of job hunting, because the paid tools are mostly wrappers around the same models. The advantage was never the tool; it's the specific, constrained prompts.
